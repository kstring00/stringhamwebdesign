# 5-minute user test: the landscaping owner

Run this with one local business owner who has never seen the site. You watch
and take notes; you don't help, explain or defend. If they ask you something,
say "What would you do if I weren't here?"

## Before you start (1 minute)

- Use their phone if they'll let you; otherwise hand them yours. Most
  visitors will be on a phone. Do a second run on a laptop if you can.
- Open a private browser tab on the live home page:
  `https://www.stringhamwebdesign.com/`
- Have this sheet and a pen ready, plus a timer.

## Read this to them, word for word

> "You own a small landscaping business and you want a better website. Using
> only this site, find out three things: what it would cost, what you'd get
> to see before paying anything, and how to contact Kyle. Think out loud as
> you go: say what you're looking at, what you're wondering, and what you'd
> do. There are no wrong answers; I'm testing the website, not you."

Start the timer. Don't say anything else until they've done all three, or
until 5 minutes are up.

## What to watch for

Write down the time and what they said or did at each moment.

| Moment | What to note |
|---|---|
| First 5 seconds | Can they say what this is, who it's for and where? Ask afterwards if you missed it. |
| The first move | Do they tap "Get a quote + free demo", scroll, or open the menu? On a phone, do they notice the Call · Text · Get a quote bar at the bottom? |
| The free demo | Do they understand, without help, that a quote comes with a demo of their homepage before they pay? Where did they get it: the hero, the pricing card, or the form heading? |
| The work | Do they swipe or click through the carousel? Do they open a demo? Do they read the labels ("Design demo · Not launched") and what do they make of them? |
| The price | Do they find "$2,000 starting"? Do they notice "fixed in a written quote"? Do they read what Website Care is for? |
| Hesitations | Any pause longer than about 3 seconds, a frown, or "hmm". Note where on the page it happened. |
| Back and forth | Scrolling up again usually means something didn't answer a question. Note what they went back to. |
| Trust | Anything they say about the photo, the phone number, League City, or "is this a real person?" |
| Contact | Which do they pick: the form, Call or Text? If the form, which field do they stop at? Do they say what they expect to happen after sending? |
| Giving up | If they'd leave, where and why, in their words. |

## After 5 minutes, ask (about 2 minutes)

1. "In one sentence, what does this person do?"
2. "What would a website cost you, and what happens before you pay?"
3. "Would you send this person your details? Why, or why not?"
4. "What almost stopped you?" (or "What did stop you?")
5. "Was anything confusing, or did anything feel like a sales pitch?"

## Afterwards

- Write the three biggest hesitations at the top of the sheet, each with
  where on the page it happened.
- If they mention wanting reviews, more examples, or the out-of-state area
  code, mark it: those are the open items (testimonials with permission,
  more live work, a local number).
- One owner is a signal, not a verdict. Run it with three before changing
  the page, and change what at least two of them stumbled on.

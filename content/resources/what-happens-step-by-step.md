# What happens, step by step

Seven steps, four phases, one fixed price. Here's the whole thing from the day you send the brief to the day you have all the logins.

You'll see this same progress bar inside your client portal, so you always know where we are.

---

## Phase 1 — Discovery
*Steps 1 to 3 · about 3 to 5 days*

### 1. You send the brief
Seven questions on my site, about five minutes. What you do, who you serve, what the site needs to make happen, and what you already have.

I reply within one business day with a straight answer on whether I'm the right fit. Sometimes I'm not, and I'll tell you.

### 2. We talk
Thirty minutes, by phone or video. What your business actually does, who your customers are, and what the site has to do for you — get calls, take bookings, sell things. We also cover what you already have: logo, photos, copy, domain, Google Business Profile.

### 3. You get a written scope
Page list, features, timeline, price, and what's not included. Every item in writing.

A 50% deposit reserves your build slot. The same day, you get your portal login.

---

## Phase 2 — Design
*Step 4 · about one week*

### 4. Content in, homepage direction out
First I collect your content — copy, photos, logo files. This is where projects stall, so I'll chase you for it, kindly and often.

Then I design the homepage direction: layout, type, color. Just the homepage. You see it in the portal, we look at it on the same screen, and you approve it before I build a single other page.

This is the step where big changes are cheap. After it, the direction is locked.

---

## Phase 3 — Build
*Step 5 · one to three weeks depending on package*

### 5. I build it
The approved direction applied across every page. Your real copy and photos in. Mobile checked on an actual phone. Forms and booking wired up and tested with a real submission.

Every ten hours of work, a timesheet posts in your portal: date, what I did in plain language, hours. Your price is fixed at the quote — the timesheet is so you can see where the time went.

Revisions happen here. Gather your notes into a list and send them together; you'll get better results and faster turnaround.

---

## Phase 4 — Launch
*Steps 6 and 7 · two to three days*

### 6. Pre-launch checks
Before anything goes live I run the same checklist on every site: speed on a phone, nothing broken at small screen sizes, every link clicked, search titles and descriptions on every page, contact form actually delivering, security certificate working, no leftover placeholder text.

### 7. Go live and hand off
Domain pointed, site live, every account in your name. Google Search Console submitted and your Google Business Profile linked.

You get one document with every login, and a short screen recording showing you how to make basic edits. The final 50% is due within seven days.

And I'll ask you for a testimonial that day, while it's fresh.

---

## After launch

The site is yours and it keeps running whether or not we ever speak again.

If you want changes later, send them over and you'll get a quote in writing before I start. Ongoing support is available — details on the [pricing page](/pricing).

---

## What could slow this down

Being honest about it, since it's usually one of these:

- **Content.** Photos and copy arriving late is the number one reason a three-week build takes six.
- **Feedback in pieces.** Ten separate emails take longer than one list of ten items.
- **Scope changes.** New pages or features added mid-build get quoted separately and push the timeline. That's not a penalty — it's just what adding work does.

None of these are a problem. They're just the things worth knowing up front.

# The words I'll use

Web people throw around a lot of terms and rarely stop to explain them. Here's every word you're likely to hear from me, in plain English, with what it actually means for you.

You don't need to memorize any of this. It's here so that when I say "I'm pointing your DNS at Vercel," you know nothing weird is happening.

---

## Your address

### Domain name

Your web address. `harbordental.com`. That's it.

You don't buy a domain once and own it forever — you rent it, usually about $15 a year. As long as you keep paying, it's yours and nobody else can have it.

**What you do about it:** Buy it on your own card, in your own name. I'll walk you through it or do it on a screen share with you, but it should never be in my name. This is the single most important thing you own.

### Registrar

The company you rent the domain name from. GoDaddy, Namecheap, and Cloudflare are all registrars.

Think of it like the county clerk's office for web addresses. They keep the record that says this name belongs to you.

**What you do about it:** Keep the login somewhere safe, and don't let the renewal lapse. If a domain expires, someone else can grab it, and getting it back ranges from expensive to impossible.

### DNS

The system that connects your address to the actual building.

Your domain name is just a name. DNS is the phone book that says "when someone types harbordental.com, send them over here." When I "point your DNS," I'm updating that phone book entry.

**What you do about it:** Nothing. I handle it. It takes a few minutes to change and up to a day for the whole internet to catch up, which is why we plan launches for a morning and not a Friday night.

## Where your site lives

### Hosting

The land your site sits on. Somebody's computer, somewhere, is holding your website and handing it to people when they visit.

Every site on the internet is hosted somewhere. There's no such thing as a site that just floats.

### Vercel

The hosting company I use. This is where your site actually lives and gets served from.

I use Vercel because it's fast everywhere in the world, it handles security certificates automatically, and it keeps a copy of every version of your site — so if something breaks, I can put yesterday's version back in about thirty seconds.

**What it costs:** About $20 a month for a business site. This is on your account and your card, not mine.

**What you do about it:** Nothing day to day. The account is in your name so you're never locked out.

### GitHub

Where your code lives, and where every change to it is recorded.

Imagine a filing cabinet that keeps every draft of every document forever, with a note on each one saying what changed and when. That's GitHub. It's how I work safely — if a change goes wrong, we roll back to the version before it.

**What you do about it:** Nothing. But the account is yours, and at handoff the whole thing transfers to you. That's what "you own the code" means in practice: it's sitting in your GitHub account, and any developer on earth can pick it up.

### Repository (or "repo")

One project inside GitHub. Your site is one repo.

You'll only hear this word if I slip into developer-speak. Feel free to ignore it.

## Safety and trust

### SSL and HTTPS

The padlock in the address bar. It means the connection between your visitor and your site is scrambled so nobody in between can read it.

If a site doesn't have it, browsers put a "Not secure" warning in front of it, and Google ranks it lower.

**What it costs:** Nothing. It's included and it renews itself.

### Backups and rollbacks

Because every version of your site is saved in GitHub and Vercel, there's no single moment where your site can be lost. "Rolling back" means putting a previous version live, and it's quick.

## Getting found

### Google Business Profile

The box that shows up on the right side of Google when someone searches your business name, with your hours, phone number, photos, and reviews.

For a local business this is often more valuable than the website itself. It's free.

**What you do about it:** Claim it if you haven't. I'll link it to your site and make sure the details match.

### Search Console

A free Google tool that shows what people typed to find you, and tells you if anything on your site is broken from Google's point of view.

**What you do about it:** Nothing at first. I set it up in your name and show you where it is.

### Analytics

Numbers about your visitors — how many, where from, what pages they looked at.

Honest note: most small businesses look at analytics twice and never again. What usually matters more is a simple question to every new customer: "how did you hear about us?"

## Taking money

### Stripe

The company that processes card payments if your site sells something.

The money goes from your customer, through Stripe, into your bank account. It never touches me.

**What it costs:** Roughly 2.9% plus 30 cents per transaction. So a $100 sale nets you about $96.80.

**What you do about it:** Set up the account in your name with your bank details. I'll sit with you while you do it.

## The build itself

### Scope

The agreed list of what I'm building. Pages, features, timeline, price.

This exists to protect both of us. It's why I can hold a fixed price, and it's why you know what you're getting.

### Revision round

One pass of your feedback, gathered together, applied at once.

Gathering your notes into one list and sending them together is much faster than sending them one at a time, and you'll get better results from it.

### Preview link

A private web address where you can see the site before it's live. Real site, real behavior, but nobody can find it unless you send them the link.

You'll get one of these early and you can share it with anyone whose opinion you want.

### Responsive

The site rearranging itself to fit whatever screen it's on — phone, tablet, laptop.

More than half of your visitors will be on a phone. Every site I build is designed for the phone first and checked on a real one before launch.

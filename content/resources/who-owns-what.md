# Who owns what

The short version: **you own all of it.**

The longer version is worth reading, because "you own your website" is something a lot of companies say and very few mean. Here's exactly what that looks like on a project with me.

---

## The rule I work by

Every account connected to your business is opened in your name, on your email, with your card. I get invited in as a helper. Not the other way around.

That means there is never a moment where firing me costs you your website.

---

## What you own, line by line

| Thing | Whose name it's in | What it costs you | Who pays |
|---|---|---|---|
| Domain name | Yours | About $15/year | You, directly to the registrar |
| Hosting (Vercel) | Yours | About $20/month | You, directly to Vercel |
| The code itself | Yours | Included in the build | — |
| GitHub account | Yours | Free | — |
| Business email | Yours | About $7/user/month if you want it | You |
| Stripe (if you sell) | Yours | 2.9% + 30¢ per sale | Taken from each sale |
| Google Business Profile | Yours | Free | — |
| Photos and copy | Yours | Included or supplied by you | — |
| Logo files | Yours | — | — |

Nothing in that table routes through me. I never hold your money, your domain, or your access.

---

## What "you own the code" actually means

It's not a figure of speech. At handoff:

- The full project transfers into **your** GitHub account.
- The hosting project sits in **your** Vercel account.
- You get a document with every login, in one place.
- I stay on as a collaborator only for as long as you want me there.

**The practical test:** you could hand this site to any developer in the world tomorrow, and they could pick it up and keep working. No proprietary builder, no locked platform, no permission needed from me.

That is not true of Wix, Squarespace, GoDaddy's builder, or most agencies. On those, you're renting a site that stops existing the day you stop paying, and the code was never yours to take.

---

## What happens if we part ways

Whether it's a year from now or a week after launch, the answer is the same: **nothing happens to your website.**

It keeps running. It's on your hosting, under your domain, paid for by you. I remove my access, hand over anything you don't already have, and that's the end of it. No exit fee, no hostage situation, no "we'll release your files once you settle the account."

I'd rather you stay because the work is good than because leaving is painful.

---

## The one thing I keep

Permission to show your site in my portfolio and to mention that I built it. That's included in every project, and it's how a small shop like mine grows.

If there's a reason you'd rather I didn't — you're in a sensitive field, or you just don't want it public — tell me and it's not a problem.

---

## What I ask from you

Because everything is in your name, a few things are genuinely your responsibility:

- **Keep your logins.** I'll give you all of them in one document at handoff. Put it in a password manager or somewhere you won't lose it.
- **Don't let the domain lapse.** Turn on auto-renew. An expired domain is the one mistake that's hard to undo.
- **Keep the card on file current.** If the hosting card expires, the site can pause.

That's the whole list. Everything else is mine to worry about.

# Business is already good. Do I really need a website?

Maybe you've been open for years. Your phone rings. Customers send their friends. You've done all of this without a website, or with one you haven't touched since it was built.

So it's a fair question: **if things are going well, what's the point?**

Here's the honest answer. A website doesn't create your reputation. You did that. A good website **catches** it, so the people who hear about you actually end up calling you instead of someone else.

---

## 1. Word of mouth doesn't skip the internet anymore

Most small businesses say referrals are their #1 source of customers. In LocaliQ's 2026 survey, 83% of owners named referrals as their top way of getting new business.

But think about what happens *after* someone gets the referral. Their friend says, "Call Maria, she's great." What do they do before calling?

They look you up.

- **97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses** (BrightLocal, 2026).
- The average person checks **six different review sites** when choosing a business.
- **AI tools** like ChatGPT are now the third most-used way people get recommendations, behind Google and Facebook. Those tools pull from what's online about you, including your website.

Your referral gets you on the list. What they find when they search decides whether you stay on it.

> **The part you never see:** when someone looks you up, finds nothing (or an outdated page with old hours), and calls the next place instead, you don't get a missed call. You get nothing. Those lost customers are invisible.

---

## 2. What a website that "converts" actually means

"Converts" just means: **a visitor does the thing you want.** They call, book, request a quote, or show up.

A website that converts isn't the flashiest one. It's the one that:

- **Loads fast**, especially on a phone
- **Says what you do, for whom, and where** in the first few seconds
- **Answers the questions people would otherwise call to ask**
- **Makes the next step obvious**: one big button, not ten options

## 3. What you can realistically expect

Here's how to think about it in plain numbers.

Unbounce found the median page in their study converts **6.6%** of its visitors. Those were mostly marketing landing pages, so treat it as a ballpark, not a promise. Your number depends on your industry, your prices, and how people find you.

## 4. Other reasons owners with steady business get one

- **Your time.** If you answer the same five questions on the phone every day (hours, prices, do you serve my area, do you take my insurance), a good site answers them for you.
- **You're not renting your front door.** Facebook, Instagram, and Yelp can change their rules, bury your posts, or lock your account. Your website and domain are yours.
- **Referrals can dry up.** If one big source of customers moves away or retires, a website keeps new people finding you.
- **It makes the business worth more.** If you ever sell or hand it down, a business that can be found online is easier to keep running.

---

## 5. What a website *won't* do

I'd rather be honest than oversell:

- **It won't fix a business problem.** If the service isn't good, a website just helps more people find that out.
- **It won't flood you with traffic overnight.** Showing up on Google takes months. Your website mostly helps the people who are already looking.
- **Nobody can guarantee results.** Google controls rankings and customers make their own choices. Anyone promising "#1 on Google" or "double your sales" is guessing.

What a good website *will* do is make sure that when someone is ready to choose you, nothing gets in the way.

---

## 6. How to tell if your website is working

You don't need fancy analytics. Ask three questions each month:

1. **How many people called, booked, or filled out the form from the site?**
2. **What do new customers say when you ask, "How did you find us?"**
3. **Is anything out of date?** Hours, prices, photos, staff.

If the first number is going up and the third answer is "no," it's working.

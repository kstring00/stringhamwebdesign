# Getting a custom website built? Here's everything I'll need from you

You've decided it's time for a real website. Good news: you don't need to know anything about design, code, or "SEO." That's my job.

What I *can't* do without you is know your business. The best websites aren't the flashiest ones. They're the ones that answer a customer's questions before they ask and make it easy to say yes. That information lives in your head, so this guide is about getting it out.

**One promise up front:** rough is fine. Bullet points, voice memos, photos straight off your phone. I'll turn it into a polished site.

---

## 1. The basics

The facts customers look for first:

- Business name, exactly how you want it written
- Phone number and email for customers
- Address, or the areas you serve
- Hours (including seasonal or holiday changes)
- How long you've been in business

## 2. What you offer

- Every service or product you want on the site
- Prices, if you want them shown (even "starting at" works)
- **The one or two things you want *more* of.** This is the most important answer on this page. It tells me what the site should push hardest.

## 3. Your customers

This is where a website goes from "nice" to "it brings in business." Tell me:

- Who your best customers are
- The questions you get asked over and over
- What makes people hesitate before they buy or book
- Why people pick you over the place down the street

Every one of these becomes a spot on your site that answers a concern right when a visitor has it, like a good salesperson standing in your store.

## 4. Your story

A few sentences on why you started and what you care about, plus a photo of you or your team if you're comfortable. People buy from people. A real face beats a stock photo every time.

## 5. Your logo and photos

- **Logo:** the original file if you have it (ask whoever designed it). If all you have is a screenshot, that's okay. Send it anyway.
- **Photos:** real photos of your work, your space, your team, your products. Phone photos are great. Take them in daylight, hold the phone steady, and send the originals, not screenshots.
- **Videos:** anything short you've posted on social media counts.

No photos yet? Tell me. We'll plan around it.

## 6. Proof that you're good

- Reviews and testimonials (screenshots are fine)
- Licenses, certifications, insurance
- Awards, press, or groups you belong to

## 7. What should a visitor *do*?

Every page needs one clear next step. Pick yours:

- Call you
- Book an appointment
- Request a quote
- Buy something
- Visit in person

Then tell me where messages from the site should go (which email or phone).

## 8. Your accounts (invite, don't share)

Some things I'll need access to. Not your passwords. **An invite with my own login**, which you can remove anytime.

- **Your domain** (yourbusiness.com): if you already have one, tell me which company it's with. If you don't have one yet, I'll walk you through getting one **in your own name**. *(Full guide: [How to buy your own domain name](/blog/how-to-buy-your-own-domain).)*
- **Your Google Business Profile:** add me as a **Manager**, not an Owner. That way I can help you show up on Google Maps while you stay in charge.
- **Social media links:** just the links. I don't need logins.
- **Tools you already use:** booking, online ordering, payments, newsletters. Just tell me the name.

**Please never send me:** passwords, credit card or bank details, or your personal email login.

## The quick checklist

| What | Example | Rough is OK? |
|---|---|---|
| Basics | Name, phone, hours, service area | Yes |
| Services | List + what you want more of | Yes |
| Customers | Top questions and hesitations | Yes, voice memo is perfect |
| Story | A few sentences + a photo of you | Yes |
| Logo and photos | Original files, phone photos | Yes |
| Proof | Reviews, licenses, certifications | Screenshots are fine |
| Next step | Call, book, quote, buy | Just pick one |
| Accounts | Domain, Google listing (invite me) | You stay the owner |

Don't have everything? Start anyway. Most people don't, and we fill the gaps together.

---

## And then we go from there

1. **We talk for about 30 minutes.** I ask questions, you talk about your business. No tech talk.
2. **I build your homepage first.** You see it before anything else gets built, so we agree on the direction early.
3. **I build out the rest** using what you gave me, written in plain language your customers understand.
4. **You review everything.** Tell me what feels off. It's your business, and the site should sound like it.
5. **We launch.** I connect your domain, test every form and button on phone and desktop, and make sure Google can find you.
6. **Everything stays in your name.** Your domain, your Google listing, your accounts. A website should be something you own, not something you rent from your designer.

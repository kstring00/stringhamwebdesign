# How I build websites that turn more visitors into customers

Most small business websites are built to *look* good. Mine are built to do a job: **turn the people who visit into people who call, book, or buy.**

That's a big claim, so here's exactly how I do it. No secret formula. Just a process, in order.

One honest note first. I can't control how many people find you on day one. Google decides rankings, and customers make their own choices. What I *can* control is what happens once someone lands on your site. That's where most businesses quietly lose customers, and that's where I focus.

---

## Step 1: Decide the one thing your site should get people to do

Before I design anything, we answer one question: **what's the one action that matters most?**

- A plumber wants a phone call.
- A salon wants a booking.
- A storage facility wants a unit reserved.
- A bakery wants an order for pickup.

Every page gets built around that one action. When a site asks visitors to do ten things (follow us, sign up, read our story, browse our gallery), they usually do none of them.

## Step 2: Find out why customers hesitate

People don't leave a website because it's ugly. They leave because they have a question or a worry the site didn't answer.

My degree is in psychology, and this is the part I care about most. Before building, I figure out what makes your customers pause:

- What do people ask you on the phone over and over?
- What do your reviews (and your competitors' bad reviews) say?
- What makes someone choose you, and what makes them go elsewhere?

Usually it's things like: *How much will this cost? Do you serve my area? Can I trust you? What happens after I call?*

## Step 3: Answer each worry right where it shows up

Think about a good salesperson in a store. They don't hand you a pamphlet at the door. They answer your question the moment you have it.

A website should do the same:

- **Price questions** get answered next to the button, not buried on another page.
- **"Do you serve my area?"** gets answered right where people decide to book.
- **"What happens after I fill this out?"** gets answered right under the form: "I'll text you back within 24 hours."

Each answered worry removes one more reason to leave.

So I design the phone version first, then the desktop. That means:

- **Fast loading.** Google's research found the chance someone leaves goes up 32% when a page takes 3 seconds to load instead of 1. I compress every image and keep pages light.
- **Thumb-sized buttons** that are easy to tap.
- **Call and text buttons that stay on screen** as people scroll, so the next step is always one tap away.

## Step 5: Write like a person, not a brochure

Phrases like "innovative solutions" and "unparalleled quality" don't sell anything. Plain words do.

In that same Unbounce study, pages written at a 5th–7th grade reading level converted at **11.1%**, about twice the **5.3%** of pages written at a college level. So I write in short sentences, in your customers' own words, and I tell them exactly what you do.

## Step 6: Put proof right where doubt shows up

**97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses** (BrightLocal, 2026). People want proof before they trust you.

So I put it where they need it:

- **Real reviews** near the button, not on a separate "testimonials" page nobody clicks.
- **Real photos** of you, your team and your work. Stock photos make people trust you less, not more.
- **Licenses, certifications and insurance** spelled out, not just implied.

## Step 7: Make the next step easy

Every extra step loses people. So I cut friction wherever I can:

- **Tap to call and tap to text** on every page.
- **Short forms.** I only ask for what you actually need. (On my own site, it's name, business, and phone *or* email.)
- **Booking and ordering** that connects to the tools you already use, instead of making people start over somewhere else.

## Step 8: Connect the other front doors

Your website isn't the only place people find you. Your Google Business Profile, Facebook, and directory listings all send people your way.

I make sure your hours, phone number and address match everywhere, and that each one links back to your site. When someone finds you on Google Maps, they should land on a page that helps them take the next step, not a dead end.

## Step 9: Measure, then improve

Launch isn't the finish line. It's where the real information starts coming in.

- I track **calls, form submissions and bookings**, the numbers that actually matter.
- I watch **anonymous recordings** of how visitors move through the site (no personal info) to see where people get stuck or give up.
- On my Website Care plan, you get a **one-page report each month** and I make improvements based on what the numbers show, one change at a time.

## What I won't do

Getting more customers should never mean tricking people. So I don't:

- Write **fake reviews** or fake "only 2 spots left!" urgency
- Use **scare tactics** or pushy pop-ups
- Promise **"#1 on Google"** or guaranteed sales numbers

Your customers should feel good about choosing you, not pressured into it.
